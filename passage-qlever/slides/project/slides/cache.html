The finding: a cache of decompressed blocks

Opening a scan decompresses a block and nothing keeps it. A nested-loop join opens one scan per outer solution and lands in the same block thousands of times running.

Fifty-eight times, and one block is enough for most of it. Per scan opened, 0.866 ms becomes 0.023 ms.

It is not an oversight upstream: a merge join never returns to a block, so for QLever's own engine a cache would be pure overhead, and large blocks are the right trade for the sequential throughput it wants. Passage cannot merge-join — a continuation has to stop mid-join and resume.

The access pattern, not the engine, is what the storage was tuned against.

Three measurements placed the cost before the cache was written, two of them negative: not the index size (0.853 ms over a million triples, 0.861 over eleven million), not the prefetch depth (0.856 to 0.860 as blocks-in-flight goes 20 to 1), not the JNI boundary (0.0004 ms to construct a cursor in C++, 0.933 for its first read). It is the first block being decompressed, every time.
