Before the triples: the Id

A permutation stores no strings, only 64-bit Ids: 4 bits of datatype and 60 bits of value — 13 datatypes defined of the 16 the tag allows.

4 bits
datatype

60 bits — a value, or an index into the vocabulary (max 2⁶⁰−1)

datatype where the value lives
Int · Double · Bool · Date · GeoPoint inside the Id — no lookup, and they compare and sort directly
EncodedVal IRIs under a configured prefix, encoded in the Id itself
VocabIndex · LocalVocabIndex · BlankNodeIndex an index into the vocabulary — a lookup

The vocabulary is split: internal in RAM (53 kB here) and external on disk (27.7 MB of words plus 8.4 MB of offsets). Ids are assigned so that their order is the strings’ order, which is what makes a prefix or a range a contiguous scan.

Published: wiki, “Internal and external vocabulary” and “QLever’s IDs for IRIs and literals” — conceptual, dated 2022, and it says the scheme “will soon be updated”. The 4/60 split is from the source.

5

The point that matters downstream: a bound component in a scan is an Id, and resolving it is a vocabulary lookup — unless it is a number or a date, which cost nothing. It is also where the absent-value trap lives: a string not in the vocabulary has no Id at all, and that has to be reported rather than silently turned into a wildcard.
